My Impact

I designed the Discover tab from scratch: one home for all the content users had been getting from different funnels, shaped by research into what they actually read. Tested as part of the redesign, it lifted new content opens by 87% and became the fullest showcase of the new brand — content, illustrations and components in one place.

Context

Nebula already had a lot of content in many formats — stories, quizzes, practices, courses and more. Users received it inside the funnel that brought them, so each person only ever saw a slice of it. The app redesign was the moment to change that.

Challenge

Turn a large, mixed library into one tab that works for everyone. Users came from different funnels with different interests, and the content came in many formats. The tab had to help each of them find something worth opening — and a reason to come back — while launching in the new brand within two months.

Behind the scenes

Starting from the data

Before designing anything, we dug into Amplitude with the content team to see how people actually used our content. A few things stood out:

  • Stories and quizzes get finished more often than any other format — so they set the order and grouping of the screen.
  • Every user group reads about love, whatever funnel they came from — so love content went to everyone, alongside the Compatibility feature.

Exploring before deciding

We studied how competitors and leading content apps build their discovery tabs and collected the patterns that work best. Using those ideas, we explored several versions of the tab — different ways to group content, card styles and block order — and compared them side by side before settling on the final structure.

Key decisions

Give every topic its own colour palette

Instead of one neutral style or no categories: each topic got its own palette, chosen to fit the brand guideline and the design system — so people recognise a topic before they read a word.

Lead with what people actually finish

Instead of ordering by format or date: stories and quizzes went first, because analytics showed users finished them most often.

Use the tab to promote high-retention features

Instead of content only: promos for Compatibility and Tarot between blocks, plus love content for every user — the topic every group read, whatever funnel they came from.

Redesign the content too, and test it in the new visual

Instead of launching the tab in the old style: the tab and the content moved to the new visual together and were tested right away — one consistent flow, and a faster move to the new brand.

New experience

One path from opening the app to reading — in the new brand all the way through.

Outcome

For the first time, users had one place for all our content — and they used it.

more opens of new content once all of it lived in one tab.

more people read content to the end when they opened it from the Discover tab.

fewer subscription cancellations among web-to-app users who opened the tab, vs. before it existed.

What’s next

After launch, we turned the results into the next round of hypotheses: other card styles and ways to categorise content, a smart search to help people find what they need faster, and splitting content into an active feed and an archive.
