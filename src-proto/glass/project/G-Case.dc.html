My Impact

I led the mobile redesign end to end, from securing team capacity to redesigning ~30 flows with 5 designers and testing most of them without a single metric loss. Underneath it, I built the design system that turned switching the whole app to the new visual into a theme change.

Context

Nebula was known as an astrology app. Marketing had launched new funnels around broader spirituality and wellness, and an independent studio delivered a new brand guideline. Users arriving from the new funnels landed in an app that still looked like the old product.

Challenge

Move the whole app to a new brand without slowing the business down. Every flow was live and earning, so no metric could drop. Product teams were busy with their own quarterly goals, so capacity for the redesign had to be found alongside them. And underneath, the old design system was full of duplicates and inconsistencies, while five designers from different teams had to ship in one visual language.

Running the redesign

From a brand book to app rules in five days

The lead designer and three of us went offsite for a hackathon, translated the studio's brand guideline into app design rules and came back with an approved MVP of the key screens.

Making the case for capacity

Every product team was busy with its own quarterly goals. Instead of competing for their time, I showed each team what the redesign gave them:

  • A fixed deadline set by marketing for the new brand — the work had to happen anyway.
  • Faster work later: moving flows one by one onto the new system would speed up their future development, with no giant release at the end.
  • A chance to test shelved ideas: each redesigned flow could carry hypotheses that had been waiting in the backlog.

Teams agreed on owners and deadlines for every flow, and dev capacity was planned alongside their roadmaps.

Five designers, one visual language

I brought in one designer from each product team and gave each of them the flows closest to their team's area, so they redesigned what they already knew best. Weekly syncs covered open questions, and regular design reviews kept five owners consistent.

Design system behind the redesign

Built from December to May, in parallel with the rollout, so each new flow could ship on it.

Audit & benchmark

Mapped duplicates and unused components, benchmarked other products' systems, aligned with devs and PMs on build time.

Two visuals, one component set

Old and new visual languages built on the same components and switched through Figma variables.

Themes by default

Old → new, light and dark: switching any screen became a mode change, for design and for dev.

iOS Liquid Glass

Researched the iOS update [and met with Apple's team], focused it where it mattered — tab bar, navigation — for a more native feel.

Key decisions

Ship flow by flow, in the order users meet them

Instead of one big-bang release: small batches between growth tests, each flow's impact read on its own.

One component set, two visual languages

Instead of redrawing screens or a separate library: both visuals on the same components, switched by variables.

Use the redesign to test new ideas

Flows that worked only got the new look. Flows that no longer fit the new positioning also got a new idea, tested in the same A/B — so we didn't have to come back and rebuild them later.

New experience

The new brand changed three things at once: how the app looks, how it speaks and what it offers. Every flow was rebuilt around all three — not just repainted.

Tarot feature

Outcome

Every mobile flow got a new-brand design, and most were already A/B-tested against the old version — none lost a core metric, and the reworked ones got better.
